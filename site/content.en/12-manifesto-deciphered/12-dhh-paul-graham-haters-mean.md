# 12.12&nbsp;—&nbsp;DHH, Paul Graham, _Haters_, _Mean People Fail_

> The Holy Book quotes two big names: **David Heinemeier Hansson (DHH)** and **Paul Graham (pg)**. These two authors aren't cited at random; each embodies a precise **rhetorical posture**. This file explains who they are, what they wrote, and why Paul Graham's _Haters_ essay (and its younger sibling _Mean People Fail_) is the direct _intellectual precedent_ of the "_First feedbacks_" chapter.

## 1. David Heinemeier Hansson (DHH)

### Identity

| Field       | Value                                                                                                            |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Full name   | **David Heinemeier Hansson**                                                                                     |
| Born        | **October 15, 1979**, Copenhagen (Denmark)                                                                       |
| Pseudo      | **DHH**                                                                                                          |
| Company     | _Co-owner_ and CTO of **37signals** (joined in 2001 as _contractor_, _co-owner_ since)                           |
| Major work  | **Ruby on Rails** (2003-2004)&nbsp;—&nbsp;Ruby web framework                                                     |
| Twitter / X | [`@dhh`](https://x.com/dhh)                                                                                      |
| Blog        | [`world.hey.com/dhh`](https://world.hey.com/dhh)                                                                 |
| Books       | _Rework_ (2010), _Remote_ (2013), _It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work_ (2018), all co-authored with Jason Fried |

### Trajectory

| Year      | Event                                                                                                                                                |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1999      | 37signals (originally a web-design studio)                                                                                                           |
| 2003      | DHH joins as a part-time contractor to write Basecamp                                                                                                |
| 2004      | **Ruby on Rails released** from Basecamp's code                                                                                                      |
| 2005      | DHH named _Hacker of the Year_ by OSCON                                                                                                              |
| 2010      | _Rework_, best-selling book                                                                                                                          |
| 2014      | _The Distributed Future of Work_ talk&nbsp;—&nbsp;pre-pandemic manifesto                                                                             |
| 2020      | _Hey_, email alternative to Gmail                                                                                                                    |
| 2021      | Public affair: internal conflict at 37signals over company policy. DHH writes his _no-politics policy_.                                              |
| 2022      | **"_I won't let you pay me for my open source_"**&nbsp;—&nbsp;explicitly refuses GitHub sponsorships                                                 |
| 2023      | **Cloud exit**. _Announces 37signals leaving AWS_ and returning to _bare metal_, saving $7M over 5 years. Launches the **ONCE** suite (self-hosted). |
| 2024-2026 | Continues publicly pamphleteering against the _SaaS gospel_, _vendoritis_, mandatory DEI, corporate conventions.                                     |

### Posture

- **Anti-cloud**: returning to a server rented at Hetzner or in a data center is _superior_ to AWS in 90% of cases. _Cloud_ is a **rent** sold as _innovation_.
- **Anti-agency-SaaS**: _Hey_, _ONCE_, and _Campfire_ are sold under _perpetual license_, not subscription.
- **Anti-monstrous-monorepo**: Basecamp is explicitly a _Majestic Monolith_, against the microservice fashion.
- **Anti-social-conformism in companies**: refuses to debate politics internally during work hours.
- **Pro-authentic Open Source**: Rails is MIT, and DHH _refuses the money_ offered to maintain it&nbsp;—&nbsp;his statement that _the code given is given_, not a product disguised as a gift.

### Why the Holy Book cites "_I won't let you pay me for my open source_"

> "_I won't let you pay me for my open source&nbsp;—&nbsp;Fuck You_"  
> &nbsp;—&nbsp;DHH, [`world.hey.com/dhh/i-won-t-let-you-pay-me-for-my-open-source-d7cf4568`](https://world.hey.com/dhh/i-won-t-let-you-pay-me-for-my-open-source-d7cf4568)

This DHH post says in substance:

1. Open source = a gift. Not a service. Not a product.
2. If you _pay_ the maintainer, you turn the gift into a transaction.
3. The transaction creates an **obligation debt**: the maintainer must maintain, do support, fix, listen to the _payer_'s needs.
4. Better to **keep it a gift** and let the maintainer keep their **freedom**.

That's exactly Ocarina's position too:

- Ocarina is **MIT**.
- There is no **GitHub Sponsors**, no **OpenCollective**, no Patreon.
- The author asks for **nothing** in exchange for the code.
- And he **keeps his total editorial freedom**.

The Holy Book invokes DHH because DHH is the only mainstream framework creator (Rails is used by GitHub, Shopify, Airbnb) to have _explicitly refused_ monetization of his gift. It's a rare precedent.

## 2. Paul Graham (pg)

### Identity

| Field           | Value                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Name            | **Paul Graham**                                                                                                                              |
| Born            | **November 13, 1964**, Weymouth (England)                                                                                                    |
| Academic career | PhD in Computer Science, Harvard, 1990. Thesis: _The State of a Program and Its Uses_.                                                       |
| Art degree      | RISD (Rhode Island School of Design)&nbsp;—&nbsp;painting                                                                                    |
| Software work   | **Viaweb** (1995, the first web-based online-store editor). Acquired by **Yahoo** in 1998 → becomes Yahoo Store. Written in **Common Lisp**. |
| Founding        | **Y Combinator** (2005, with Jessica Livingston, Robert Morris, Trevor Blackwell)&nbsp;—&nbsp;startup incubator                              |
| Written works   | _On Lisp_ (1993), _ANSI Common Lisp_ (1995), _Hackers and Painters_ (2004), 100+ essays on [`paulgraham.com`](https://paulgraham.com)        |
| Twitter / X     | [`@paulg`](https://x.com/paulg)                                                                                                              |

### Trajectory

- **Hackers and Painters** (2004)&nbsp;—&nbsp;Y Combinator's founding manifesto. Thesis: good hackers think like artists (painters), not engineers (architects).
- **Y Combinator**: incubation by _batches_ model, became the global standard. Produced Stripe, Airbnb, Dropbox, Reddit, Twitch, Coinbase, Instacart, DoorDash.
- Stepped back in 2014: handed YC over to **Sam Altman**. Keeps writing essays.
- 2018-2026: more _philosophical_ essays ("_Identity_", "_Cities and Ambition_", "_Mind the Gap_", "_Wealth_").

### Posture

- **Schemer-Lisper at heart**: still defends Common Lisp as "_the language that's 30 years ahead_" long after the fashion has passed.
- **Anti-corporate**: _Hackers and Painters_ is largely an argument against _corporate_ work. The **hacker** is defined in opposition to the _suit_.
- **Pro-founder**: the founder is always right because they _carry_ the risk. VCs who impose their direction are almost always wrong.
- **Pro-essay**: Paul Graham defends the **essay** as an intellectual form. His thesis: you _think by writing_, not before writing.
- **Anti-haters** (see _Mean People Fail_, below).

## 3. _Haters_ (Paul Graham, May 2014)

URL: [`paulgraham.com/fh.html`](https://paulgraham.com/fh.html) (lit. _"For Haters"_, abbreviated `fh.html`).

### Thesis

> _Hatred is by far the most common motive for hostile online comments._

Paul Graham distinguishes:

| Category | Description                                                          |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _Critic_ | Someone who disapproves of _what you do_ and argues it               |
| _Hater_  | Someone who _hates you personally_ and uses what you do as a pretext |

_Haters_ are recognizable:

- They are **systematically negative** about everything you do.
- They are **factually imprecise**&nbsp;—&nbsp;they invent what they don't like.
- They are **frustrated** in their own lives (low personal success, low social status).
- They _suffer_ from your existence as a **personal insult**.

### Why they exist

Paul Graham says:

1. The attacked person _succeeds_ at something (creation, _fame_, project)
2. The _hater_ is just a _fanboy_ pointed the other way

### Conclusion of the essay

> _The exhortation isn't to ignore haters, but to recognize them as a side effect of doing anything._

If you do something that works, _haters_ appear **by construction**. Their existence is the _cost_ of success, not a sign you did something wrong.

### Why the Holy Book cites _Haters_

The Holy Book's "_First feedbacks_" chapter documents precisely this kind of feedback.

Igor Casanova **refers** to Paul Graham's text because it's the **canonical precedent**: the _haters_ of Ocarina, of functional programming, of AI, of truly KISS code, are the instance of a general phenomenon already theorized by a respected author of the _hacker-founder_ scene.

## 4. _Mean People Fail_ (Paul Graham, November 2014)

URL: [`paulgraham.com/mean.html`](https://paulgraham.com/mean.html)

### Thesis

> _It struck me recently how few of the most successful people I know are mean. There are exceptions, but remarkably few._

1. The internet showed us how purely _mean_ some people can be
2. Although there's a huge mass of jerks we were previously spared from and they're now visible, you notice they don't really exist more among people who succeeded in their lives
3. The _stupid mean gnomes_ (personal rephrasing) are generally not brilliant at making a startup take off
4. Being mean pushes one to be stupid, to fight instead of doing the work, and exhausts everyone for nothing
5. Mean people don't attract the best talent&nbsp;—&nbsp;rather, people who _need a job_
6. People who want to make the world better more than they want to make money have a higher success rate
7. The world, according to him, is moving from a zero-sum game where everyone fights for scarce resources to a game where you _win_ by having good ideas and creating new things
8. He insists the environment must be healthy for one to play a constructive game rather than wage war or assume their government is there to plunder
9. Paul Graham and his wife Jessica work hard to raise their children not to be mean; they tolerate noise, mess, and junk food but not meanness. Because being mean can only lead to failure.

> "_22 years, I could never be a hater, 22 blunts in this game I'm a player._"

## 5. Side by side: DHH vs Paul Graham

| Axis                     | DHH                                                                                                                                                    | Paul Graham                                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Generation               | 1979&nbsp;—&nbsp;second-wave indie                                                                                                                     | 1964&nbsp;—&nbsp;first-wave hacker-founder |
| Reference language       | Ruby                                                                                                                                                   | Lisp                                       |
| Model                    | Founder _who executes_ (Basecamp, Hey, ONCE)                                                                                                           | Founder _who theorizes_ (essays, YC)       |
| Argumentative posture    | Pamphleteer, frontal, twitter-brawl                                                                                                                    | Essayist, distant, structural              |
| Bête noire               | The cloud, extractive SaaS                                                                                                                             | Corporate VCs, _suits_                     |
| Refuses                  | Open-source sponsorships, DEI conventions                                                                                                              | Big company, _committee_                   |
| Self-employed            | Yes (37signals = his company)                                                                                                                          | Yes (YC = his company)                     |
| Reading of the "_hater_" | _Ruby on Rails: The Documentary_ opens with: "_Let the dog barks, Sancho. It's a sign we are moving forward._" **Exactly as the Holy Book phrases it** | "_Understand the mechanism and move on_"   |

The Holy Book picks up **both** because it _is_ both: pamphleteer à la DHH if not more, essayist à la Paul Graham.
