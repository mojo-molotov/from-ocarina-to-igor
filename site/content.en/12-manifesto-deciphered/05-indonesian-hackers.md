# 12.05&nbsp;—&nbsp;Early-2000s Indonesian hackers

> The Holy Book quotes "_the first Indonesian hacker group_" without naming it in the main text, but the cited mantra ("_We Can Do All What You Can't Do_") is indeed **YogyaCarderLink (YCL)**'s. It remains to explain _why_ the Indonesian scene of that era deserves a citation in a 2026 testing framework manifesto.

## 1. The May 1998 riots

### The trigger

In May 1998, Indonesia went through a major economic crisis (fallout from the 1997 Asian crisis) and a political crisis (end of the Suharto regime). Riots broke out in Jakarta, Medan, Surakarta, notably targeting the **Indonesian Chinese minority** (merchants, middle class). Estimates: ~1000 dead, hundreds of documented rapes.

These events are historically called _Tragedi Mei 1998_ or _Mei Kelabu_ (Grey May).

### The Chinese hackers' reaction (1998-2000)

The information crossed the nascent Chinese internet within days. Hackers met on **IRC** and launched what would be recognized as **the first Chinese patriotic cyber-war**:

- Spam of Indonesian government sites.
- Ping floods.
- Creation of the **"_China Hacker Emergency Conference Center_"** to coordinate.
- Birth of the term **"_honker_" (红客)** = _red hacker_, as opposed to "_hacker_" (黑客 = _black hacker_), a patriotic, ideologically driven hacker.
- Emergence of the **Green Army** group (founded 1997, politically recognized in 1998), later **China United Green Alliance**.

Source: [Real Hack History&nbsp;—&nbsp;_History of Hacktivism in China – 1998 Indonesia Riots_](https://realhackhistory.org/2025/09/22/history-of-hacktivism-in-china-1998-indonesia-riots-chinese-patriotic-cyberwars/)

### The Indonesian hackers' reaction (1999-2005)

On the Indonesian side, the _scene_ crystallized as **counter-reply** and more broadly as **assertion of technical identity** in a country with no organized cybersec tradition.

Groups emerged in several cities:

- **Jakarta**: carding / phreaking scene.
- **Bandung**: university scene (Institut Teknologi Bandung&nbsp;—&nbsp;ITB).
- **Yogyakarta**: birth of **YogyaCarderLink (YCL)**.
- **Surabaya**, **Medan**: smaller groups, _crews_.

## 2. YogyaCarderLink (YCL)

### The two mantras

YCL signs with two distinct identity passwords that open and close its defaces:

#### `We Can Do All What You Can't Do.`

This is the **identity slogan**. Found on Zone-H signatures, forums, e-zines.

- "_We Can Do All_": claim of _universal technical competence_.
- "_What You Can't Do_": exclusion of the _other_&nbsp;—&nbsp;the industry, the government, the _normie_, the certified _professional_.

It's a **typical _underground_ motto**. The North American counterpart would be "_We Are Legion_" (Anonymous, 2008) or "_Lulz Security_" (LulzSec, 2011). It's a universal identity grammar.

#### `Don't fuck with us.`

It's the **warning slogan**. Present on many YCL deface pages and picked up more broadly as the **carding/defacement scene mantra** of the 2000s.

Ocarina's Holy Book flips it to _past indicative_:

> _**YOU FUCKED WITH US!**_

Rhetorical pivot from future (warning) to past (observation): _we warned you, you did it anyway, now own up_.

It's the logical structure of the entire "_First feedbacks_" chapter: the author spent years _politely explaining himself_, the _slipologues_ kept twisting everything, imposing their mental models, despising, calling others autistic; the verbal-explosion moment (_quote from `true colors`_ + "_YOU FUCKED WITH US!_") is the shift to _past indicative_.

The mantra is cited **literally in English** at the very end of the invective block, regardless of the source language:

> _PUTAIN D'INCOMPÉTENT QUI NOUS EMMERDE!_  
> _PUTAIN DE PARASITE!_  
> _PUTAIN D'INCAPABLE!_  
> **_YOU FUCKED WITH US!_**

The last line, in italic English, all caps, _closes_ the sequence. It's the explicit echo of the YCL mantra **`Don't fuck with us`**, transposed to past tense. The rhetorical structure governing the whole "_First feedbacks_" chapter finds its **literal signature** here.

### Why the Holy Book cites it

> _Alright: I'll quote you the motto of the first Indonesian hacker group I was exposed to as a child: "We Can Do All What You Can't Do."_
>
> _I'm the bug you'll never get rid of._

1. Autobiographical marker&nbsp;—&nbsp;the author was exposed to YCL _as a child_. Suggests an IRC-early trajectory (pre-2010), and exposure to the _international_ scene (not just the French one).
2. Inverted-humility marker: "_I'm not invoking Cap'tain Crunch or Kevin Mitnick, I'm invoking a scene no one knows_". More _underground_ than invoking LulzSec or Anonymous.
3. Southeast-Asian lineage marker&nbsp;—&nbsp;the author acknowledges an intellectual debt to _that_ scene, not to the US scene here.

## 3. Why the Indonesian scene deserves citation

### An _autonomous_ scene

The American and European hacker scenes were built on solid academic infrastructure (universities, ARPANET from 1969, Bell Labs, etc.). The Indonesian scene was built **from nothing**:

- No Indonesian ARPANET.
- Universities underequipped in the 1990s.
- Expensive, limited connectivity (dial-up modems, then Warnet cybercafés).
- No local cybersec industry at the moment these groups emerged.

Everything was learned by oneself, on IRC, in English, with poor infrastructure. Exactly the _pure underground trajectory_ the Holy Book values.

### A scene _politicized by circumstance_

The 1998 riots _forced_ the local scene to position itself:

- To respond to Chinese attacks.
- To defend national pride.
- To _exist_ technically against a state that didn't recognize their skills.

### A scene _that endured_

YCL still exists in 2026. Many American _crews_ from the 2000s have disappeared (arrests, dissolutions, aging out). The Indonesian scene endured under other names: **IndoHaxSec**, **Indonesian BackTrack Team**, **711 Elite Team** (which paradoxically defaced YCL in 2017). It's a _living_ scene.

Source: [INDOHAXSEC Indonesian Hacking Collective&nbsp;—&nbsp;Arctic Wolf](https://arcticwolf.com/resources/blog-uk/indohaxsec-indonesian-hacking-collective/)

## 4. The _technical_ nuance

YCL was originally a **carding scene**, not an _ideal-driven_ scene like LulzSec. They stole credit card numbers to _use_ (buying gear, converting to cash). YCL gradually specialized in _bank fraud_ broadly. The ethical rhetoric came _later_.

It's an **important difference** from LulzSec (which hacked for _the lulz_ and never cared about profiting financially). YCL was more pragmatic: illegal practices to _live_, in a tough economic context. _Like part of the Russian hacker scene, by the way._

## 5. Lineage with Ocarina

| YCL                                                            | Ocarina                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Autonomous scene, poor infrastructure                          | A single runtime dep, auditable code, apparently a single person with no particular means     |
| Politicization by circumstance                                 | Politicization by the _slipologue_ industry                                                   |

Doing what others can't do, _precisely because they refuse to do it_.

That's exactly what Ocarina claims. The ROP DSL, strict typing, refusal of pytest and its plugins: "_They don't even know what ROP means_", says the Holy Book. _"We can do all what you can't do."_

## 6. Chronology note

The Holy Book says "_the first Indonesian hacker group I was exposed to_". _First_ is relative to the author's path, not absolute. The _very first_ Indonesian scene (1996-1999) was rather centered in Bandung (ITB) and Jakarta. YCL is rather a **second wave** (from 2001-2005 onward).

For someone exposed via IRC at the time, YCL was _probably_ the first Indonesian _crew_ to appear; their deface signatures were recurrent on Zone-H back then.
