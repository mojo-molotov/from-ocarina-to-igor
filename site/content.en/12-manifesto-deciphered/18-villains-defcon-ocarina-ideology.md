# 12.18&nbsp;—&nbsp;"_Villains but not Monsters_", DEF CON, "_live as a white hat or die as a black hat_", and Ocarina's ideology

> Ocarina is neither an aesthetic whim nor an out-of-the-blue UFO. It's the technical expression of a **coherent ideology** inherited from the hacker movement: the **Villain, but not a monster** ethic, the **tribal culture** of the conferences (DEF CON foremost), and YTCracker's **stage motto**: "_live as a white hat, or die as a black hat_".

## 1. "_Villains_"

### Why this word

The word **villain** (English; _vilain_ in French) is neither an insult nor a compliment _stricto sensu_. It's a **reclaimed identity**:

| Institutional reading                   | Hacker reading                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Villain = outlaw character in the story | Villain = one who _refuses_ the role assigned to him                                 |
| Villain = bad guy                       | Villain = one who _acts_ while others _wait for an authorization that never arrives_ |
| Villain = to be neutralized             | Villain = one who produces _real change_ the system doesn't                          |
| Villain = bad example                   | Villain = intelligence-agency model                                                  |

The classic _stigma_ flip: take the insult and turn it into a name worn with pride&nbsp;—&nbsp;same mechanic as _queer_ reclaimed by the LGBT community, _punk_ reclaimed by the 1976-78 scene, _ghetto_ reclaimed by 1990s hip-hop.

### Why this identification

1. **Hackers were penalized by default** as soon as they _acted_ technically.
2. **Institutions refused** to acknowledge the value of their warnings, so acting _outside_ institutions became necessary.
3. The identity "_passive good citizen_" is on offer for _normies_. For hackers, that's an **unacceptable** behavior.
4. The identity "_villain_" is honest: you _are_ what the system identifies as threat, and you _accept_ that label.

## 2. "_Villains but not Monsters_"

### The ethic

There's a **morally non-negotiable** boundary:

| Villain                            | Monster                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Breaks a system to _reveal_ it     | Breaks a system to _destroy_ a person       |
| Steals data to _expose_ wrongdoing | Steals data to _profit_ at victims' expense |
| Hacks a powerful corporation       | Harasses a vulnerable individual            |
| Confronts a powerful actor         | Persecutes a weak one                       |
| Targets an _abuse of power_        | Enjoys an _abuse of vulnerability_          |
| Takes personal risks               | Makes others carry the risk                 |
| Codes out of ethics                | Codes out of cynicism                       |

This distinction **structures** the scene. True enthusiasts **hate** monsters, even more than _normies_ do.

### Why harassers are _hated_ by real hackers

1. **They themselves were harassed**. The 1985-2005 hacker cohort was massively victim of school harassment. _Reproducing_ harassment on someone else, especially vulnerable, is the **behavior** they swore to fight.
2. **Harassers damage the whole scene**. When a _swatting_ causes a death, _the whole scene_ gets blowback.
3. **Villain ethics include _honor_**. The villain picks his targets with **rigor**. The monster doesn't make this distinction. He acts for the _pleasure_ of harm, by **pathological sadism**.

### How far the scene detects monsters

Hackers developed a **whole range** of tools and reflexes to spot infiltrated monsters:

| Tool / reflex                 | Application                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Honeypots**                 | Fake servers / fake accounts / fake _data leaks_ that bait opportunists. If someone bites and uses it, that's a signal. |
| **Social engineering**        | Testing newcomers.                                                                                                      |
| **Deepfakes**                 | Today, using generated artifacts to trap fraudsters (deepfake of oneself to see who tries to use it).                   |
| **Malwares**                  | Markers planted in shared tools to trace who passes them to whom.                                                       |
| **Phishing**                  | Build a fake phishing to trap aspiring scammers.                                                                        |
| **Snitches / Feds detection** | Compulsive reading.                                                                                                     |

### Extreme tribal loyalty

The real-hacker scene is **fiercely loyal** to its _verified_ members and **fiercely distrustful** of every newcomer.

| Trait                      | Manifestation                                                                              |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Long admission tests**   | A new member can stay _lurking_ for 2-3 years.                                             |
| **Vouching system**        | To be introduced to a circle, you need a _vouch_ (endorsement) from an established member. |
| **Absolute loyalty**       | Once admitted, you cover your _brothers_.                                                  |
| **Brutal excommunication** | A betrayal earns a _permanent ban_.                                                        |
| **Tribalism**              | _My crew above all_.                                                                       |

It's exactly the mechanic of _post-traumatic_ communities: scene veterans operate with an emotional intensity reminiscent of **military veterans**'. Strong internal cohesion + extreme external suspicion.

## 3. The _tooling_ culture

### The _tool_ as fundamental product

| Tool                                          | Made by                                     | Distribution                                |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Nmap** (Fyodor, 1997)                       | 1 person (Gordon Lyon)                      | Free, GPL, clear docs, rigorous _man pages_ |
| **Metasploit** (HD Moore, 2003)               | Small team, Rapid7 acquisition but OSS core | Free                                        |
| **Wireshark** (Gerald Combs, 1997-98)         | Small team, then large community            | Free, GPL                                   |
| **John the Ripper** (Alexander Peslyak, 1996) | 1 person                                    | Free                                        |
| **Hashcat** (Jens Steube, 2009)               | 1 person, then small team (Gristina)        | Free, MIT (open source since 2015)          |
| **sqlmap** (Daniele Bellucci, 2006)           | Small team                                  | Free                                        |
| **Burp Suite** (Dafydd Stuttard, 2003)        | Stuttard's personal tool, then PortSwigger  | _Free_ + paid pro version                   |
| **Aircrack** (Christophe Devine, 2004)        | 1 person                                    | Free, GPL                                   |
| **Aircrack-ng** (T. D'Otreppe, fork 2006)     | Fork of **Aircrack**, small team            | Free, GPL                                   |
| **theHarvester, recon-ng, Maltego CE**        | Small teams                                 | Free or freemium                            |
| **OllyDbg, IDA Free, Ghidra (NSA, 2019)**     | Various                                     | Free / open source                          |
| ...                                           | ...                                         | ...                                         |

1. **Small team or single author at the start**.
2. **OSS or freemium**.
3. **Detailed technical documentation**.
4. **Reputation built on quality**, not promotion.
5. **_Peer-to-peer_ distribution** (conferences, forums, GitHub, IRC).

What's called the _tooling culture_ is the conviction that **good technical tools make good _hackers_**, and that **a good, well-written tool is worth a thousand PowerPoint presentations**.

### Why this is central for Ocarina

Ocarina **is** a _tool_ in that sense:

- Single author.
- MIT, technical documentation.
- GitHub distribution + free Holy Book.
- Reputation to be built on **quality**, not promotion (no marketing site, no _landing page_ with CTA).
- _Not a product, not an offer_.

That's the **metaphysical distinction** Ocarina carries. All modern e2e frameworks (Cypress, Playwright, Robot Framework) are **sold as products**. Ocarina is **given**. The _morality_ is different.

## 4. DEF CON&nbsp;—&nbsp;the annual _shrine_

### Description

| Field         | Value                                                                                                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Name          | **DEF CON**                                                                                              |
| Founder       | **Jeff Moss** (alias _The Dark Tangent_)                                                                 |
| First edition | **1993**, Las Vegas                                                                                      |
| Festival name | DEF CON 1, DEF CON 2, ... DEF CON 32 (August 2024), DEF CON 33 (August 2025)                             |
| Current venue | Las Vegas Convention Center (LVCC), summer                                                               |
| Volume        | ~30,000 attendees (DEF CON 32)                                                                           |
| Entry fee     | _Cash only at the door_, ~$460 (DEF CON 32)&nbsp;—&nbsp;no online ticketing, anti-tracking               |
| Anonymity     | No _name badge_. The badge is an _electronic object_ with hardware puzzle (`#badgelife`)                 |
| Tracks        | _Talks_, _villages_ (Car Hacking, ICS, Bio Hacking, Lockpicking, AI, etc.), _CTF_, _wargames_, _parties_ |

DEF CON is **the** annual global meet of the hacker scene. It's:

- **A scientific production venue**: many _0day_ presentations happen there (sometimes after _responsible disclosure delay_, sometimes not).
- **An interpersonal identification venue**: it's where you _put a face_ on pseudos.
- **A _rite of passage_ venue**: a first DEF CON is an initiatory rite for many.
- **A _balance_ venue** between corporate _white hat_ and underground _black hat_: each rubs shoulders with the other openly.
- **A tribal _validation_ venue**: elders recognize elders.

### DEF CON codes

- **No photos of others without explicit permission**: _doxing prevention_ is cultural.
- **No _shop_ talk** in monitored open spaces.
- **Cash only**: no banking trail.
- **Anti-Feds chant**: historic, partly humorous, partly serious. _Spot the Fed_ is an unofficial _game_ where attendees try to identify undercover federal agents.
- **CTF (Capture The Flag)**: hacking competitions.
- **Badgelife**: each edition produces an electronic badge with hardware puzzle, hackers turn it into _a collectible_ and _modding_ object.

### YTCracker at DEF CON

YTCracker has been performing regularly at DEF CON since **at least 2011**. His sets are _iconic_ moments.

## 5. "_Live as a white hat, or die as a black hat_"

- _White hat_: ethical hacker, working _with_ institutions (bug bounty, authorized pentests, responsible _disclosure_, defensive security).
- _Black hat_: offensive hacker without an ethical framework, who exploits for profit, vengeance, or _lulz_, without consideration for victims.
- _Grey hat_: intermediate zone, acting outside legal bounds for ethically defensible ends.

At some point in a hacker's trajectory, **you have to choose**&nbsp;—&nbsp;persisting as _black hat_ _statistically_ ends in prison, dead, or broken.

### Biographical meaning

YTCracker lived it:

- **1999**: NASA hack (black hat, around 17).
- **2005-2017**: Digital Gangster (grey zone).
- **2015+**: corporate cybersec consulting, DEF CON conferences, Darknet Diaries, _Shawn Ryan Show_ (white hat).

His own trajectory **embodies** this motto. He survived because he made the turn in time. Others in the same cohort didn't and are **dead or imprisoned**.

## 6. Why all this is an **ideology**, not a whim

### Definition of ideology

An _ideology_ is a **coherent system**:

1. of **axioms** (about humans, power, technique, value),
2. of **values** (what to defend, what to fight),
3. of **practices** (how to act daily),
4. of **institutions** (the structures one builds or refuses),
5. of **narratives** (founding myths, heroes, enemies).

### Values

| Defends                      | Fights                 |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Small auditor-friendly repos | Opaque megamachines    |
| MIT + gift                   | Vendor lock-in         |
| ISTQB / business vocabulary  | Jargon, bullshit       |
| Sovereign author             | Design committees      |
| _Underground_ community      | _Corporate influencer_ |
| Veterans                     | _Slipologues_          |

### Practices in Ocarina

### Conclusions

It's a complete **system**. Not an aesthetic whim, not diffuse paranoia, not a _UFO_.

The _UFO appearance_ comes from the fact that the combination _strict typing + ISTQB vocabulary + pamphlet aesthetic + SaaS refusal + AI-first + LulzSec quotes_ **exists nowhere else**. It's unique because it derives from a **deep identity**, a specific trajectory.

The ideology behind Ocarina is the **sum** of this heritage.
